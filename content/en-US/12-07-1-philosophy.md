**Wu wei**, often rendered “non-action,” sits at the center of classical Daoist thought. It does not mean doing nothing. It means not forcing, not over-managing, and not fighting the way water already wants to flow.

A cook whose knife finds the joints, or a ruler who governs lightly, is the usual image. The paradox is practical: sometimes the skilled move is the one that **adds the least** extra effort.
