For years, huge amounts of encrypted internet traffic relied on just a few standard **1024-bit Diffie-Hellman groups**. That created a myth of diversity: many sites looked independent, yet deep down they often depended on the same mathematical parameters.

If an attacker invested enough effort to precompute one of those shared groups, the payoff could apply far beyond a single target. In security, variety is sometimes less real than it appears.
