Rotokas, spoken in Papua New Guinea’s Autonomous Region of Bougainville, is famous among linguists for an unusually small phoneme inventory. Its practical alphabet is often listed as twelve letters, with no need for the crowded consonant chart of English.

A language can still tell stories, argue, and joke with a lean sound system. Complexity lives in grammar and use, not only in how many symbols fill a keyboard.
