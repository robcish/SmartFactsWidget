Britain's **Statute of Anne** (1710) is often called the first copyright statute in the modern sense. It granted authors, not only stationers' companies, a right to print for a **fixed term**, then let the work enter a wider market.

The law still served publishers, and later acts stretched the clock. Even so, the idea that culture could be exclusive for a while and then shared is the legal skeleton under today's copyright debates.
