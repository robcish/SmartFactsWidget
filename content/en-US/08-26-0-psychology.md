During the **1997 match**, Kasparov described Deep Blue as an **"alien opponent"** — something fundamentally different from any human player he had faced. The machine's unexpected moves and lack of visible emotion unsettled him deeply.

After losing, Kasparov reversed course and began dismissing the computer, famously saying it was *"as intelligent as your alarm clock."* Psychologists note this shift as a classic **ego-defense mechanism** — by minimizing the opponent, he could reframe the loss as a fluke rather than a genuine defeat by a thinking machine.
