title: Copyright protects expression, not ideas
teaser: The law usually covers how something is expressed, not the bare concept behind it.
published: true
version: 1

Copyright law generally does not give someone ownership over an abstract idea by itself. What it protects is a particular expression: a text, image, melody, or other creative form fixed in a tangible way.

That distinction matters because culture depends on reuse and variation. People are free to build on broad concepts, but not simply copy someone else's concrete work.
