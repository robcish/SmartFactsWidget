Alan Turing's 1936 paper *"On Computable Numbers"* introduced the concept of a **Turing machine** — an abstract device that formalizes what it means to compute. Using it, he proved the **halting problem**: there is no general algorithm that can decide, for every possible program and input, whether that program will eventually stop or run forever.

This was more than a curiosity. The halting problem established a hard boundary on what computers can do, no matter how powerful. It also showed that Hilbert's *Entscheidungsproblem* — the dream of a mechanical procedure to decide all mathematical truths — was **impossible**.
