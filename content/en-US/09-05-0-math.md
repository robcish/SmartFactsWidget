Supersingular isogeny key exchange, or **SIKE**, was one of the most intriguing post-quantum cryptography candidates because it relied on advanced algebraic structures called isogenies. Then, in 2022, researchers broke it with a surprisingly efficient classical attack.

That was a humbling moment for cryptography. A scheme built to survive future quantum computers turned out to be vulnerable to ordinary present-day mathematics.
