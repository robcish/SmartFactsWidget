In May 1974, Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn published *"A Protocol for Packet Network Intercommunication"*, describing a system that would let incompatible networks talk to each other. The core idea was a **two-layer design**: TCP handled reliable delivery while IP handled routing across networks.

That December, **RFC 675** used the word "internet" — short for *internetwork* — for the first time in an official document. The concept was revolutionary: instead of building one giant network, you could stitch together thousands of independent ones. By 1983, ARPANET switched fully to TCP/IP, and the modern Internet was born.
