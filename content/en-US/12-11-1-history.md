After World War I, farmers in Western Australia faced drought, low grain prices, and thousands of **emus** moving through fences into wheat. The government sent a small army detachment with Lewis guns in what newspapers nicknamed the Emu War.

The birds scattered, guns jammed, and the campaign was quietly dropped. It remains a lesson in how a military tool can fail against mobile wildlife and a public hungry for a simple victory.
