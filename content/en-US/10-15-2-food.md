A fresh, ripe cranberry has springy internal air pockets, so it can **bounce** instead of thudding flat. That odd trait once made simple mechanical sorting surprisingly effective.

Berries that bounced well were often in better condition, which turned a quirky physical property into a practical tool for food handling.
