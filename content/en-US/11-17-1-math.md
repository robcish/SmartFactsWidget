The birthday paradox feels wrong because people instinctively compare themselves with everyone else one at a time. The real comparison is the number of possible pairs inside the group, and that number grows surprisingly fast.

By 23 people there are 253 different pairs, which is enough to push the chance of a match above half. Probability often surprises us because our intuition is poor at counting combinations.
