Umami receptors were first identified on the human tongue in **2000**, confirming what Japanese chemist Kikunae Ikeda had proposed nearly a century earlier — that savory was a distinct fifth taste. Six years later, researchers found the same receptors in the **stomach lining**, suggesting the gut can "taste" amino acids directly.

The stomach receptors appear to trigger digestive responses when they detect glutamate, helping the body prepare for protein-rich food before it even reaches the intestines.
