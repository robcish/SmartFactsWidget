Garum, the fermented fish sauce of ancient Rome, delivered a strong **umami** taste long before scientists identified glutamate. It was made by letting fish and salt break down into a liquid seasoning packed with savory compounds.

Romans used it across social classes, from ordinary cooking to elite banquets. In flavor terms, it played a role surprisingly close to modern fish sauce or soy sauce: small amounts could transform an entire dish.
