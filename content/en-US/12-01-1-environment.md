In spring and summer, snowfields can turn watermelon-pink. The color comes from **snow algae** (often *Sanguina* or related green algae) packed with the carotenoid **astaxanthin**, which shields the cells from intense light and cold.

Darker snow reflects less. That drop in **albedo** lets the surface absorb more energy, so melt can run faster than on clean white snow. Hikers sometimes notice a faint sweet smell, but the bloom is a living film, not fruit juice.
