For centuries Western science recognized only four basic tastes: sweet, sour, salty, and bitter. In 1908, Japanese chemist **Kikunae Ikeda** challenged that model. He noticed that dashi broth made from **kombu seaweed** had a savoury depth that none of the four could explain.

Ikeda isolated the molecule responsible — **glutamic acid** — and named the taste *umami*, roughly meaning "pleasant savoury taste." Western scientists remained sceptical for nearly a century, until **2002** when researchers identified dedicated umami receptors on the human tongue, finally confirming it as a legitimate fifth basic taste.
