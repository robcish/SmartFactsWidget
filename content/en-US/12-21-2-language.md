**Ubykh**, once spoken near the Black Sea, is famous among linguists for an extreme consonant system, often counted around eighty, against a tiny set of vowels. Speakers stacked contrasts of place, secondary articulation, and ejectives that English simply does not use.

The last fluent speaker, Tevfik Esenç, died in 1992 after years of work with researchers who raced to record the language. What remains is a reminder that a community can carry a sound world that does not map onto familiar alphabets.
