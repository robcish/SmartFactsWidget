For centuries, the symbol & was sometimes treated as the 27th character in the English alphabet. When children recited it at the end, they said "and per se and," meaning roughly "and, by itself, means and."

Over time that phrase blurred in speech and became ampersand. It is a reminder that punctuation marks can have biographies too.
