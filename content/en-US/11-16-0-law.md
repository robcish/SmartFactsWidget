In Miranda v. Arizona, the U.S. Supreme Court held that suspects must be informed of key constitutional rights before custodial interrogation. The ruling turned legal doctrine into a scripted warning recognized around the world.

Its wording can vary, but the core idea is the same: a confession should not come from ignorance of the right to silence or counsel. A courtroom principle became part of popular culture.
