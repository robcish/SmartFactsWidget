The English word **bankrupt** is commonly traced to Italian *banca rotta*, a “broken bench.” Money changers laid out coins on a wooden stall. If a dealer failed, the story goes, the bench was broken so nobody would trade there again.

Whether every town staged that drama, the image stuck. Modern bankruptcy is a legal process, not street furniture, but the older picture still explains why failure is spoken of as something that **snaps**.
