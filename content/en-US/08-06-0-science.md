In 1975, Gregory Chaitin defined the halting probability **Ω** — a real number between 0 and 1 that represents the probability that a randomly chosen program will eventually stop running. Each bit of Ω encodes the answer to an independent halting question.

What makes Ω remarkable is that it's **algorithmically random**: no pattern or shortcut exists for computing its digits. You can approximate it from below, but every new digit requires solving an entirely new undecidable problem. It's the mathematical embodiment of irreducible complexity.
