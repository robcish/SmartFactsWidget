French chef Auguste Escoffier created dishes rich in **savory depth** in the late 19th century, even though the word "umami" did not yet exist in Western cooking. He repeatedly concentrated flavor with stocks, meat extracts, and slow reductions.

That makes him a useful bridge between culinary practice and food science. Cooks had already learned how to intensify umami long before chemistry explained why those dishes tasted so full.
