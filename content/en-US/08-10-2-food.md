Chemist Kikunae Ikeda coined the term **umami** in 1908 after isolating glutamate from kombu seaweed broth. He combined "umai" (うまい, delicious) with "mi" (味, taste) to describe the savory depth that didn't fit sweet, sour, salty, or bitter categories.

Despite Ikeda's discovery, Western science resisted accepting umami as a basic taste for nearly a century. It wasn't until **2002** that researchers identified specific tongue receptors (mGluR4) for glutamate, finally granting umami official status as the fifth fundamental taste alongside the traditional four.
