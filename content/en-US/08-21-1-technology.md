By the late 1990s, **Unix and Unix-like systems** ran on over **90%** of the machines on the TOP500 list of the world's fastest supercomputers. The combination of portability, networking, and multi-user design made Unix the natural choice for high-performance computing.

Today that dominance continues under the **Linux** banner — a Unix-like system that took over the TOP500 entirely. Since November 2017, every single supercomputer on the list runs Linux, including the current fastest machines exceeding **one exaflop** of performance.
