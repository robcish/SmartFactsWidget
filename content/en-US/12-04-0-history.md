In 1901, sponge divers off the Greek island of **Antikythera** recovered a corroded lump of bronze from an ancient shipwreck. Inside were dozens of **gears**. The device, dated to roughly the 2nd or 1st century BCE, modeled the zodiac, lunar phases, and eclipse cycles, including the Moon’s uneven motion.

Nothing of similar complexity is known from the ancient Mediterranean. It sat in a museum for years before X-rays revealed a machine that looked, to modern eyes, like a clockwork sky.
