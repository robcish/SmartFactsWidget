Diffie and Hellman introduced public key exchange in 1976, and a U.S. patent followed in **1977**. That timing mattered because it placed a foundational cryptographic idea inside the legal world of licensing and intellectual property.

The story also became messier later, when declassified work showed that British intelligence researchers had discovered similar ideas earlier in secret. In cryptography, legal priority and scientific priority do not always line up neatly.
