A **cashew** is a seed hanging under a swollen stem called the cashew apple. Between the shell and the kernel sits an oil rich in **anacardic acids**, cousins of the irritants in poison ivy.

That is why “raw” supermarket cashews are typically steamed or roasted before they reach a snack bowl. The apple itself is eaten fresh in growing regions, even while the nut needs careful industrial handling.
