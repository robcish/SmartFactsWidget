Sea otters often crack hard-shelled prey by striking it with a stone or smashing it against one. Some individuals repeatedly use a preferred rock and even store it in a loose fold of skin under the forearm.

Tool use is rare enough in animals to attract special attention. In otters, it turns a floating meal into a small demonstration of learned technique.
