title: The Turing Test checks for conversation, not intelligence

In his 1950 paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," Turing replaced the question "Can machines think?" with a practical experiment: a human judge converses with both a machine and a human via text. If the judge can't reliably tell them apart, the machine passes.

Turing called it the **Imitation Game**, not the "Turing Test" — that name came later. He predicted that by the year 2000, machines would fool 30% of judges after five minutes of conversation. What he didn't anticipate was that modern chatbots would pass this bar while still lacking common sense, reigniting debates about whether the test measures intelligence or merely linguistic mimicry.
