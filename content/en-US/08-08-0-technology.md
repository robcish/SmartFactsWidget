The Stochastic Neural Analog Reinforcement Calculator (**SNARC**) simulated 40 neurons using 3,000 vacuum tubes and the mechanical clutch system from a B-24 bomber's autopilot. Minsky was just a 24-year-old graduate student at Princeton when he built it.

SNARC learned by reinforcement: it modeled a virtual rat navigating a maze, strengthening connections when the rat moved toward the exit. The machine actually worked — the simulated rat learned to solve the maze. It was decades ahead of its time, but vacuum tube technology made it impractical to scale.
