British mathematician Clifford Cocks invented what we now call RSA encryption while working at **GCHQ in 1973**, four years before Rivest, Shamir, and Adleman independently published their version at MIT. Because Cocks' work was classified, he received no public credit for decades.

The British discovery remained a state secret until **1997**, when GCHQ finally declassified the documents. By then, the "RSA" name was firmly established and the MIT trio had already commercialized the technology.
