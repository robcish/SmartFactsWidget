A placeholder for an empty place value is not quite the same as a number that can be added, subtracted, and reasoned about abstractly. That distinction is one reason zero arrived unevenly across mathematical traditions.

Once accepted, it became indispensable not only for arithmetic, but for algebra, calculus, and computing.
