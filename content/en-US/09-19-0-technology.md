In 1936, Alan Turing described an abstract device now called the **Turing machine**. It reads symbols step by step from a tape and follows a fixed set of rules.

The idea mattered because it showed how a very simple machine could represent general computation. Modern computers are built very differently, but the concept still anchors computer science.
