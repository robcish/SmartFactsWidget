Black walnut trees produce **juglone**, a chemical that can interfere with growth in surrounding plants. Gardeners notice it because tomatoes, apples, and other sensitive species may wilt or fail near the tree.

This is a classic example of allelopathy, in which one plant changes the local biology of another through chemistry. A forest or garden is not just a crowd of neighbors but an active biochemical contest.
