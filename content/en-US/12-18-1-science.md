When a surface is far hotter than water’s boiling point, a droplet can ride on a thin film of its own vapor. That hovering is the **Leidenfrost effect**, named after an 18th-century doctor who watched water dance on a scorching spoon.

The vapor layer is a poor conductor, so the drop lasts longer than on a merely hot pan. It is why a splash in a too-hot skillet can skitter instead of hissing into nothing.
