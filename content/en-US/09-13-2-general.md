title: Dennard scaling gave computers a free speed era
teaser: For years, smaller transistors also meant cooler and faster chips. When Dennard scaling broke down, automatic speed gains largely ended.
published: true

Dennard scaling predicted that shrinking transistors would keep **power density roughly constant**, allowing chips to run faster without overheating. For a long time, that gave computing a kind of free lunch: each generation often improved speed without demanding major software changes.

Around the mid-2000s, that pattern weakened. Since then, many gains have come from multicore designs, parallelism, and specialized hardware instead of just higher clock rates.
