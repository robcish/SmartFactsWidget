When Douglas fir trees are attacked by insects, they can transmit **chemical warning signals** through mycorrhizal fungal networks to neighboring ponderosa pines. The recipient trees then ramp up production of **defense enzymes** before the insects even reach them.

This underground communication system — sometimes called the **"Wood Wide Web"** — demonstrates that forests are not collections of competing individuals but interconnected communities. The fungal threads linking the trees can span dozens of meters, creating a biological information network beneath the soil.
