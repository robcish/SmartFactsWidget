In experiments, people who built a simple box later bid **more** for it than for an identical box built by someone else. Psychologists Michael Norton, Daniel Mochon, and Dan Ariely named this the **IKEA effect**.

Effort becomes a story of competence. The finished object carries that story, even if the hex key left a few extra holes. The bias helps explain homemade gifts, and why we keep furniture that is only almost level.
