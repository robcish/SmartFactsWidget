In Edo-period Osaka, feudal domains stored tax rice in warehouses and issued **rice bills** that merchants bought and resold. By the late 1600s those notes were trading in Dojima; in **1730** the shogunate authorized a spot market and a book-settled futures market with membership and clearing rules.

Traders could lock in a rice price without moving sacks of grain. Price signals even traveled inland by courier and flag. Historians treat Dojima as the first **institutional futures exchange**.
