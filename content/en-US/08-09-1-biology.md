title: Trees trade nutrients through a fungal internet underground

Suzanne Simard's landmark **1997** experiment used radioactive carbon isotopes to demonstrate that paper birch and Douglas fir trees were actively exchanging carbon through shared **mycorrhizal fungi** in the soil. The fungi connect root systems of different trees, forming a vast underground network.

Through these networks, older "mother trees" can funnel sugars to shaded seedlings, and stressed trees can receive resources from healthier neighbors. The system even carries chemical warning signals about insect attacks. A single fungal network can connect dozens of trees across species boundaries, challenging the view of forests as collections of competing individuals.
