On May 11, **1997**, IBM's **Deep Blue** defeated world chess champion Garry Kasparov **3½–2½** in a six-game match in New York City. It was the first time a computer beat a reigning world champion under standard tournament conditions.

Deep Blue could evaluate **200 million positions per second** thanks to custom chess chips, but it was Kasparov's psychological unraveling — particularly after a mysterious move in Game 1 — that many analysts credit for the outcome. The match became a cultural milestone in the debate over human versus machine intelligence.
