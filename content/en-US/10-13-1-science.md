Bananas contain potassium, and a tiny fraction of that potassium is the radioactive isotope **potassium-40**. That makes bananas slightly radioactive in the most ordinary, natural way.

The dose is extremely small and harmless in everyday life, which is why the banana equivalent dose became a popular way to explain how tiny some radiation exposures are.
