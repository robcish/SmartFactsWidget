**Chinese characters** (hanzi) originated over 3,000 years ago for a single language, yet they spread across East Asia to write languages with completely different grammars and phonologies. Japanese uses them as **kanji** alongside two native syllabaries; Korean employed them as **hanja** for centuries before Hangul became dominant; and Vietnamese used a modified set called **chữ Nôm** until the Latin-based quốc ngữ replaced it.

The appeal was cultural prestige and the ability to convey meaning across language barriers — a Chinese and Japanese reader could grasp the same character even if they pronounced it differently. Today, only Chinese and Japanese still use the characters in everyday writing.
