The genus **Genlisea**, known as corkscrew plants, has developed one of the most unusual carnivorous strategies in the plant kingdom. Instead of flashy traps above ground, they hunt **underground** with modified leaves shaped like a Y.

Each arm of the Y spirals into a corkscrew lined with **inward-pointing hairs**. Microscopic organisms like protists and nematodes can enter but cannot back out — the hairs act as one-way turnstiles, funnelling prey toward a digestive chamber at the junction. Some Genlisea species also hold the record for the **smallest known plant genomes**, with as few as 63 million base pairs.
