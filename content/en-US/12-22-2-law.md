In **Ashford v Thornton**, an accused man demanded **wager of battle**, an old procedure in which a dispute could be settled by armed combat. The judges found that the medieval option had never been fully repealed, even though almost nobody still used it.

Public shock followed. In 1819 Parliament abolished appeals of murder and trial by battle, closing a legal fossil that had survived into the industrial age.
