Zero began as a placeholder in Indian mathematics before developing into a number in its own right. That shift made positional notation vastly more powerful, because the same digits could represent very different values depending on where they stood.

Without zero, long multiplication, algebra, and later computing would all be much clumsier. A tiny circle helped turn arithmetic into a compact language.
