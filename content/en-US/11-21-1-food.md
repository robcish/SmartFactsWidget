Ripe cranberries have small air pockets inside, which makes them buoyant and slightly elastic. That is why harvested berries can float in flooded bogs and also bounce on sorting equipment.

Producers have even used simple bounce tests to separate firmer fruit from damaged batches. It is a handy example of how plant structure affects both farming and food processing.
