The **1943** paper "A Logical Calculus of Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity" showed that neurons could be modeled as binary threshold units. McCulloch was a neurophysiologist and Pitts a self-taught mathematical prodigy — he was just 20 years old.

Their key insight: a sufficiently large network of these idealized neurons can compute any function a Turing machine can. This was the first paper to bridge logic, computation, and brain science, directly inspiring both artificial intelligence and the later development of artificial neural networks.
