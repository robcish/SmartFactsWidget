In 2006, Martin Hellman suggested that the protocol often called **Diffie-Hellman** should really be named Diffie-Hellman-Merkle. His point was that Ralph Merkle's work was crucial to the economic and conceptual birth of public-key cryptography.

Names matter in science because they shape who gets remembered, cited, and funded. A short rename proposal can reveal a much bigger question about how credit gets priced in technical history.
