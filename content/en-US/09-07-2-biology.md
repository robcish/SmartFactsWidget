title: Douglas firs share more carbon with kin
teaser: Douglas firs linked by fungal networks tend to send more carbon to close relatives. The pattern hints at plant kin recognition.
published: true
version: 1
---
In forests, roots and fungi can form **mycorrhizal networks** that move nutrients and chemical signals between plants. Experiments with Douglas firs suggest that more carbon may flow between closely related trees than between distant ones.

Researchers interpret that pattern as a sign that plants are not entirely passive neighbors. Even without brains, they may respond differently to relatives sharing the same underground network.
