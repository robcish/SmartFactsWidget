The **Unix philosophy** — write programs that do one thing well, and connect them using pipes — emerged from Ken Thompson and Dennis Ritchie's work at Bell Labs in the early 1970s. It was a radical departure from the monolithic software designs of the era.

This idea that complex behavior should arise from **combining simple tools** rather than building one giant program influenced everything from the Linux command line to modern microservices architecture and the API-driven web. Doug McIlroy, who invented Unix pipes, summarized it: *"Write programs that do one thing and do it well."*
