title: Forests trade nutrients through fungi
teaser: Mycorrhizal networks can move carbon and chemical signals between plants.

Fungi attached to plant roots form underground networks that help move water and nutrients through the soil. In some cases, those networks also appear to pass along chemical warnings or shift resources between connected plants.

Scientists still debate exactly how much these networks coordinate forest life, but their role is clearly more active than once assumed.
