In **1980**, computer scientist Edward Fredkin established a **$100,000 prize** at Carnegie Mellon University for the first computer program to defeat a reigning world chess champion in a regulation match. At the time, most experts believed it would happen within a decade.

The prize went unclaimed for **17 years** until Deep Blue's victory over Kasparov in 1997. The money was split between the members of the Deep Blue team. Fredkin had also created smaller prizes for intermediate milestones — $5,000 for the first computer to achieve master rating and $10,000 for grandmaster rating.
