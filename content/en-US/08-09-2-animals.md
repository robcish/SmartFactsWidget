title: Chimp fingerprints can fool human forensic experts

Chimpanzees share roughly **98.7%** of our DNA, and their fingerprints reflect this closeness. They have the same loop, whorl, and arch patterns humans do, with similar ridge density and minutiae points — the very features forensic experts use for identification.

In practice, only subtle statistical differences in ridge spacing distinguish the two. This similarity has been cited in forensic literature as a potential source of misidentification at crime scenes near primate habitats. Koalas are the only other non-primate mammals known to have fingerprints convergently similar to ours.
