In the early 1770s, young Johann Wolfgang von **Goethe** fell deeply for Charlotte Buff, who was engaged to another man. Rather than collapse under the pain, he poured it into his 1774 novel *The Sorrows of Young Werther*.

The book became a sensation across Europe and is often cited as a textbook case of **sublimation** — the psychological defence mechanism where unacceptable emotions are redirected into socially valued activities. Goethe later said the novel saved his life by externalizing feelings he could not resolve. Ironically, the book triggered a wave of copycat suicides across Europe, a phenomenon now called the **Werther effect**.
