A placeholder for nothing seems obvious now, but zero was once controversial in parts of Europe. Numeral systems without a true zero made calculation and bookkeeping much more cumbersome.

The spread of Hindu-Arabic numerals gradually changed that. Once zero became standard, it made positional notation vastly more powerful and helped open the door to modern algebra and computation.
