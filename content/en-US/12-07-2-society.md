Around **930**, chieftains on a new settler island began meeting each summer at **Þingvellir**, a rift-valley plain. That gathering, the **Althing**, heard laws spoken aloud, settled disputes, and later became a symbol of a society that tried to govern by assembly rather than a king on the spot.

The modern Icelandic parliament still bears the name. Continuity was interrupted under foreign rule, then restored, but the idea of an open-air national meeting is unusually old.
