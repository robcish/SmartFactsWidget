Unlike most reptiles, a **crocodile's tongue** is almost completely attached to the roof and floor of its mouth by a membrane. This means it cannot protrude, move food around, or lick its lips.

So how does a crocodile eat? It relies on powerful jaws and a **"death roll"** to tear prey into swallowable chunks, then tilts its head back and lets gravity do the rest. The fixed tongue does serve one purpose: a special valve called the **palatal valve** at the back of the throat seals off the airway, allowing the crocodile to open its mouth underwater without drowning.
