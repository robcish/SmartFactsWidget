The familiar warning that a suspect has the right to remain silent comes from *Miranda v. Arizona*. In that case, the US Supreme Court ruled that people in custody must be informed of key constitutional protections before interrogation.

The decision became far larger than the individual case. It turned a courtroom principle into a phrase recognized through everyday policing, television, and public life.
