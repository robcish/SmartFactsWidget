title: Stoics rehearse misfortune on purpose

Roman Stoics practiced **premeditatio malorum**, a calm preview of things that could go wrong: illness, insult, a ruined plan. The point was not to brood, but to meet a hard day without pretending surprise is the same as wisdom.

Seneca wrote that we suffer more in imagination than in reality when fear is vague. A short, honest rehearsal can shrink that fog and make an ordinary evening look like something already worth keeping.
