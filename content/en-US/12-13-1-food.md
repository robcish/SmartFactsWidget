A fig is an inverted flower cluster. Many species rely on a **fig wasp** that crawls in through a tiny opening, pollinates the flowers, and often dies inside. The fruit later digests much of the insect with enzymes.

Not every supermarket fig still contains a wasp, and some commercial varieties are parthenocarpic. The partnership still explains why a fig is less a simple fruit than a locked greenhouse with a door the size of a pin.
