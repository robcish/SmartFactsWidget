The network that became the Internet started with just **four nodes** by the end of 1969: UCLA, Stanford Research Institute, UC Santa Barbara, and the University of Utah. All were in the **western United States**, connected by 50 kbit/s leased telephone lines.

The very first message, sent on October 29 from UCLA to Stanford, was supposed to be "LOGIN" — but the system **crashed after "LO."** The full connection was restored an hour later. Within a year, ARPANET expanded to 13 nodes, and by the mid-1970s it linked sites across the country, proving that packet switching could work at scale.
