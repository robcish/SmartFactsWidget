Across Polynesia, the culture hero **Māui** is a trickster who slows the Sun, steals fire, and fishes with a magical hook. In Māori tradition the North Island is the fish he hauled up; Hawaiian tellings likewise tie islands to his catch.

The stories explain coastlines as something pulled into air, not only worn by waves. They also cast the trickster as a maker of the human-scale world, for better and for trouble.
