Occam's razor does not claim the simplest idea must always be true. It suggests that when two explanations fit the evidence equally well, the one with fewer extra assumptions is usually the better starting point.

Philosophers and scientists use it as a guide for reasoning, not a magic rule. A neat explanation can still be wrong if the world turns out to be more complicated.
