Neutron stars pack the mass of the Sun into a sphere roughly **20 km** across, with surface gravity about **200 billion** times Earth's. Despite these lethal conditions, science fiction has imagined creatures that thrive there.

In Robert Forward's *Dragon's Egg* (1980), tiny intelligent beings called **cheela** live on a neutron star's surface, experiencing time a million times faster than humans. Stephen Baxter's *Flux* places organisms inside a star's superfluid interior. These stories let authors explore how radically different physics could still produce life.
