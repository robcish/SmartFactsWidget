Unix was born at **Bell Labs in 1969** when Ken Thompson and Dennis Ritchie began building a small, clean operating system after their employer pulled out of the ambitious Multics project. They originally called it **"Unics"** — a pun on Multics, implying it did one thing well rather than trying to do everything.

The name was later respelled as "Unix." Thompson wrote the first version in assembly language for a spare **PDP-7** minicomputer, and the project grew largely because management didn't notice it was happening.
