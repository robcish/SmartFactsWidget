title: Grammatical Gender Follows a Pattern

Linguist Joseph Greenberg discovered an **implicational universal**: if a language distinguishes grammatical gender in the third person (like English "he" vs. "she"), it will always also distinguish gender in the **second person** ("you") — though the reverse is not necessarily true.

This pattern holds across unrelated language families worldwide. It suggests that gender systems develop in a predictable order, starting with distinctions closest to the speaker and expanding outward. Languages like Arabic follow this pattern perfectly, with gendered forms of "you" alongside "he/she."
