The **Haber-Bosch process** converts atmospheric nitrogen (N₂) into ammonia (NH₃) at extreme temperatures (400–500°C) and pressures (150–300 atm). It feeds roughly half the world's population, but the energy cost is staggering — about **2%** of global energy consumption each year.

Nature does the same job at room temperature: nitrogenase enzymes in soil bacteria fix nitrogen effortlessly. But the enzyme's mechanism involves quantum effects that are impossibly complex to simulate on classical computers. Quantum computers might eventually model nitrogenase precisely enough to inspire a room-temperature industrial catalyst — potentially saving 1% of all humanity's energy use.
