In 2020, researchers demonstrated a **chosen-prefix collision** against SHA-1 using rented GPU time on the cloud. The total cost: roughly **$45,000** and a few weeks of computation.

A chosen-prefix attack is far more dangerous than a simple collision because the attacker controls the beginning of both files, making it practical to forge certificates or sign malicious code. At $45,000, the attack is affordable for **organized crime groups** and intelligence agencies — but still too expensive for casual hackers. The result cemented SHA-1's status as cryptographically broken for any security-critical use.
