Most people credit ARPANET as the first packet-switched network, but the **NPL network** at Britain's National Physical Laboratory got there first. Built by Donald Davies and his team, it began operating in early **1969**, months before ARPANET's famous "LO" message in October.

Davies independently coined the term **"packet switching"** — a concept also developed by Paul Baran at RAND. The NPL network ran at **768 kbit/s**, far faster than ARPANET's initial 50 kbit/s lines. Though it remained a local research network rather than a cross-country system, its design influenced ARPANET's own protocol choices.
