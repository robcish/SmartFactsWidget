The Y2K bug is often mocked as shortsighted programming, but in the 1960s and 1970s memory and storage were expensive enough that every byte mattered. Using **two-digit years** was often an economic compromise, not pure carelessness.

That choice created a technical debt bill for the future. What looked like a tiny shortcut on one machine eventually became a global maintenance problem once software survived far longer than its creators expected.
