When AT&T began licensing Unix source code in the early 1970s, universities could obtain a copy for just **$150**. The low price — essentially a media and handling fee — made Unix accessible to computer science departments worldwide.

This bargain-priced access created an entire generation of programmers fluent in Unix. The **University of California, Berkeley** became a major hub, eventually producing **BSD** (Berkeley Software Distribution), which spawned its own family of operating systems still used today, including FreeBSD and parts of macOS.
