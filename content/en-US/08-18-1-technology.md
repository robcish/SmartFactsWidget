In 2020, a team of mathematicians successfully factored an **829-bit RSA key** (RSA-250), a feat requiring the equivalent of **2,700 CPU-years** of computation spread across a large cluster of machines. The effort used the General Number Field Sieve algorithm.

To put this in perspective, the widely used **RSA-2048** standard is estimated to need roughly a billion times more computational effort to crack — a gap that keeps current encryption safe, at least until quantum computers mature.
