Alan Turing proved in **1936** that no general procedure can determine whether an arbitrary program will terminate. But he never called it the "halting problem" — his paper discussed the *Entscheidungsproblem* and "circular" versus "circle-free" machines.

It took until **1952** for mathematician Martin Davis to coin the now-famous term in his book *Computability and Unsolvability*. The catchy name helped transform an abstract logical result into one of computer science's most recognizable concepts, widely taught in every theory of computation course.
