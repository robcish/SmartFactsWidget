Neutron stars pack more mass than the Sun into a sphere only a few tens of kilometers across. The matter inside is compressed so intensely that ordinary comparisons start to fail.

Popular explanations often say that a teaspoon of neutron-star material would weigh about a **billion tons** on Earth. The exact number depends on the star, but the point is the same: density at that scale is almost unimaginable.
